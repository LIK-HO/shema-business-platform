# v1.4 Durable Execution Plane

## Purpose

The durable execution plane is the next controlled hardening step after the approved 12-element kernel.

It does not introduce a new business domain. It makes the kernel's existing operational contracts executable and recoverable:

- durable job state in PostgreSQL;
- lease-based worker ownership;
- safe completion by the current lease holder;
- bounded retry scheduling;
- deterministic reclaim of expired work;
- idempotent job identity.

## State model

QUEUED -> RUNNING -> SUCCEEDED

Temporary failure:

RUNNING -> RETRYABLE_FAILURE -> RUNNING

Permanent failure:

RUNNING -> FAILED

Cancellation remains terminal.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. Every durable job has an idempotency key.
2. A worker must hold an unexpired lease to execute a job.
3. An expired lease makes work reclaimable by another worker.
4. A worker with an expired or missing lease cannot mark work complete.
5. Retry timing is bounded by an explicit policy.
6. Job persistence is infrastructure state; it cannot rewrite business truth.
7. Domain handlers remain idempotent and own business invariants.
8. Job state and business state are changed through the same PostgreSQL transaction boundary when they belong to one command.

## Persistence

Migration 0006 adds the canonical job_execution table and indexes for ready and expired work.

The application boundary exposes a JobRepository port. PostgreSQL implements lease-safe claim, completion and failure transitions. No worker implementation is coupled to the database schema.

## Why this comes before UI

The Web/PWA/Android layer should consume stable command/query semantics and durable job status rather than becoming responsible for retries, recovery or concurrency control.

The operator should see the result of the execution system, not its internal machinery.


## Worker orchestration

`JobRunner` is the application-level coordinator over the durable `JobRepository`.

Its contract is intentionally narrow:

`claim -> resolve handler -> execute -> complete/fail -> audit + outbox`

- Handler lookup is explicit and missing handlers fail the job.
- Handler failures are classified as retryable or permanent.
- Retryable failures are scheduled through `RetryPolicy` and stop at the attempt limit.
- Completion and failure publication share one PostgreSQL transaction.
- Worker execution is at-least-once; handlers must therefore be idempotent.
- The runner does not own business truth and does not introduce a second queue or scheduler.


## Outbox delivery

`OutboxDispatcher` is the external publication boundary for committed outbox events.

Its contract is:

`read pending -> publish externally -> mark published -> audit`

- Publication is at-least-once.
- The external publisher must deduplicate by immutable `event_id`.
- A publisher failure leaves the event pending for a later attempt.
- Marking the event published and writing its audit record share one database transaction.
- The dispatcher does not mutate domain truth and does not replace the transactional outbox.
